vpn-ui redesign / sheet set 07

The inbound rail

Rail was chosen off sheet set 06. This set asks what the rail carries. Five variants, drawn at the exact dimensions of the sibling they have to match: the client add/edit modal, 820px wide with a 200px rail, its summary block, and its icon tabs.

The same five fixtures as before, so a variant can be judged on VLESS at 195 fields and on SSH at 25 without changing pages.

00

What "in sync" actually means

Measured off client_membership_modal.html and components.css, not approximated.

The client modal already solved this shape. Its rail is not a list of links: it is a summary block that reports the account at a glance, then four icon tabs. On Add it has nothing stored to report, so it previews what is being typed rather than sitting empty. Every variant below inherits that.

That last one is not a detail. "Security (REALITY)" and "Quota and billing" both overflow a 200px rail once an icon and a count sit beside them, and an ellipsis in a nine-item list reads as a defect rather than as truncation. Section names for the rail are therefore short names, and the full title lives on the pane heading where it is actually useful.

Held against that spec, the Rail on sheet 06 was close but not in sync. What it got wrong, and what these five fix:

  • No icons. The client rail gives every tab one (user, cluster, key, dashboard).
  • No summary block. The rail's top third is the account at a glance, and it previews on Add. The inbound has an equivalent worth reporting: what it listens on, how many accounts ride it, what it has moved.
  • Wrong widths. 960px dialog and a 232px rail, against the sibling's 820 and 200.
  • Wrong field layout. Side by side labels, where the client modal stacks them.
  • Counts and state. Not in the sibling, but the inbound needs them: it is the form with 9 sections and a save gate, where the client has 4 and no gate. This is the one place worth diverging, and three of the five below test different amounts of it.

Icons are only proposed here after being rendered against the bundled antd.min.js and confirmed to produce a glyph. Two obvious candidates, node-index and partition, do not exist in this build and are not used.

01

Twin

The client rail, unchanged. Summary block, icons, flat list.

The baseline the other four are measured against: no counts, no state, no groups. If an operator can work the inbound form with exactly the affordances the client form gives them, nothing else here is worth its cost.

820px / rail 200pxclick a tab

What it shows: the summary carries the inbound at a glance, and on Add it previews the remark being typed exactly as the client rail does. Where it breaks: VLESS has nine tabs against the client's four, and with no counts they all look equally weighty. Final mask reads the same as Identity, but one holds 64 fields and the other 5.

02

Twin with state

Same rail, plus the two things the inbound has and the client does not.

A field count on every tab, and a dot on any section holding a reason Save is disabled. The count is what tells an operator that Final mask is not a two-minute detour; the dot is what stops the silent gate from sheet 06, fact 03. Sections that are advanced and untouched sit dimmed.

820px / rail 200pxcounts and blocker dots

Cost: two spans in the tab template and a per-section validity map. It is the smallest possible divergence from the sibling and it buys the most. The risk is visual noise: nine rows each carrying an icon, a label, a number and sometimes a dot is a busy 200px.

03

Grouped

Listener, Security, Policy. Structure instead of a longer list.

Nine flat items is a list; nine items under three headings is a shape. The groups are stable across protocols even as their contents change, so an operator who learns that certificates live under Security keeps that knowledge when they move from OpenVPN to VLESS.

820px / rail 200pxthree fixed groups

Cost: it is the one variant that visibly departs from the client rail, so the two modals stop looking like the same control. It also pays for structure with height: on SSH the three headings introduce more chrome than the four tabs under them are worth. Judge this one on SSH, not on VLESS.

04

Spec rail

The summary block reports the outcome, not the inputs.

The client rail summarises the account because that is what an account is: a name, a quota, a deadline. An inbound is not a noun in the same way. What an operator actually wants confirmed is what the server will run and what the customer will receive, which is Split's idea from sheet 06 folded into the rail so no second pane is needed.

820px / rail 200pxlive outcome in the summary

Cost: the listener line and the artifact have to be derived live, and the share link is already generated twice in this codebase (the JS model and Go). A preview that drifts from what saving produces is worse than no preview, so this variant carries a correctness obligation the other four do not.

05

Adaptive

Show what this protocol needs. Find the rest by name.

The rail shows the sections that are on the default path and folds the rest behind one disclosure, so SSH shows four rows and VLESS shows six rather than nine. Above them is the control that the 195-field case really wants: a filter that searches field names, not section names, and answers the question an operator actually has, which is where Padding Placement lives.

820px / rail 200pxtype in the filter, it is live

Cost: the most build of the five. It needs a per-protocol notion of which sections are on the default path, and a searchable index of every field label, which today exists only as markup inside 24 partials. It is also the only variant that helps at 195 fields as much as it does at 25.

If you want a recommendation: ship 02, then add 05's filter when the field index exists. 02 is the client rail plus a count and a dot, which keeps the two modals recognisably the same control while admitting the one real difference between an account and an inbound: an inbound can be unsaveable, and it should say so in the rail rather than in a greyed button. 04's summary is the better content for that block and can replace 01's at any point without touching the tabs; treat it as a second step, not a fork in the road.

Against grouping: 03 reads best on VLESS and worst on SSH, and SSH is not the rare case. Sixteen of the 24 protocols have four or five sections. Grouping optimises the tail.
