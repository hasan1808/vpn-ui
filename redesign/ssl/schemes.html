Five ways to rebuild the SSL manager

The certificate machinery underneath is sound: an immutable version store, a stable active path, a live reloader that swaps a certificate without dropping a connection, a rate ledger, an adoption path for whatever deploy.sh left behind. None of that is in question here. What is in question is the page in front of it, which currently asks the operator thirty-three questions to answer one.

Two words for one thing, and neither is the thing anyone wants

The page says profile and certificate for the same object, and asks you to name it before you have one. The operator did not come here to own certificates. They came to make the panel stop showing a browser warning.

Four validation choices for a question the server can answer itself

Whether port 80 is free, whether the name resolves here, and whether what you typed is an IP address are all things this box can find out in well under a second. Asking the operator to pick HTTP, webroot, DNS or IP is asking them to do the probe by hand.

Re-apply and Renew look identical and neither says what will happen

One contacts a CA and one does not. One can be rate limited and one cannot. One may bounce a service and one will not. The page shows two grey buttons a centimetre apart.

Verdict

The largest drop in vocabulary of the five. An operator never learns what a profile is, because there are none. The mental model is the same one they already hold: the panel needs HTTPS, the subscription server needs HTTPS.

Risk: a certificate an inbound serves belongs to no slot, so it lives only in the list at the bottom and the two cards say nothing about it. That is honest but it makes the third consumer a second-class citizen.

Recommendation

Build 01 as the page, and 02 as what its buttons open

They are not competitors. Scheme 01 is a good answer to where am I and a thin answer to how do I get one. Scheme 02 is the reverse. Two slots as the resting state, the three questions as the flow that both Secure this and New certificate open, and the certificate list from 01 at the foot of the page for adoption and for the multi-certificate case.

That combination lands at roughly 15 controls, down from 33, needs one new field on the existing preflight response and no new endpoints, and keeps the one thing scheme 01 alone would lose: inbounds as a place a certificate can go.

If the priority is shipping this week instead, scheme 03 is the honest choice. It is front end only, reuses the cards already merged on this branch, and can be done in one pass without touching Go at all.
