vpn-ui / clients / second attempt

Five designs, not five containers

The first set was one design in five wrappers. These five differ in what the view is: a wallet you leaf through, a manifest you paste, a status board, a thing you compose and send, and a history you read. Each one also answers "then what is More Information" in its own way.

00

The account these are drawn over

One customer, six inbounds, four kinds of credential

Same fixture in all five, chosen so nothing is flattered: k7m2x9qp4, 47.2 of 120 GB used, 21 days left, on six inbounds that between them cover every artifact shape the panel can produce. Two of the six are quietly broken, which is the point.

Four facts from the code that every design below has to survive:

  • More Information already shows a lot. Today's modal carries transport, email, id, status, usage, created, updated, last online, comment, the IP-limit log, Remained/Total/Expire, the Telegram id, the subscription and JSON links, and the share links. The gap is not missing fields, it is that all of it is one membership at a time: this account needs six visits to see itself.
  • The subscription URL does not cover everything. GRE gets no subscription entry at all, and the credential VPNs appear only as a fabricated trojan:// connection card that carries the endpoint and quota but cannot be dialled.
  • Some artifacts are fetched, not computed. WireGuard, AmneziaWG, SSH and GRE come from the backend per membership, and IKEv2 fetches its Remote ID separately.
  • Protocols are not colour-coded, on purpose. The panel has one accent and four status hues, and protocol tags are deliberately neutral. So none of these five tells protocols apart by colour; where they need to be distinguished it is by texture, position or wording.
What was wrong with the first five. They all rendered the same thing: an identity header, a four-stat strip, a subscription block, then a stack of per-membership cards. Only the container changed. A container is not a design, and picking between five of them is not a decision worth making.
01

Wallet

Credentials are objects you leaf through

Six memberships, six cards in a stack. You leaf down them and the one you pick comes forward at full size with its artifact as the hero: a QR at 150px, or the two .conf files, or the credential block. The stack stays visible so you never lose the sense of how much the customer has.

Cards are told apart by texture, not colour: a link card is smooth, a config card is ruled like a document, a credentials card is lined like a form, and a card with nothing to give is hatched and obviously empty. That reads at a glance without spending a hue the panel has reserved for status.

More Information is the back of the card. Flip it and the same card shows the technical readout: transport, security, fingerprint, SNI, flow, uuid. Same object, other side. No second window and no second button to explain.
Try: leaf through the stack then Flip on any card
CostsBreaks
Front-end. One card component with a front and a back, plus four artifact renderers. An account on twelve inbounds is a stack you scroll. The flip is an animation to get right, and a card back is one more place a field can hide.
02

Manifest

The whole account as text you can paste

No cards, no tiles. One monospace readout of the entire account, the way the panel's own terminal already talks. Everything is selectable, the whole thing fits one screen, and it is already the right shape to drop into a ticket or a chat.

This is the operator's answer, and it is the only design here that is faster than the table it was opened from. It also composes: links, conf and tech filter the same readout rather than opening anything, so there is one surface and no navigation at all.

More Information is a filter, not a modal. The tech verb prints the per-membership technical block inline, in place, in the same readout. The button on the row opens this view with that verb already applied.
Try: the verbs along the bottom each one rewrites the readout
CostsBreaks
Cheapest of the five. One text renderer, and terminal.css already exists in the panel. Unsendable to a customer, and hostile to anyone who does not already know the vocabulary. QR codes have nowhere natural to live. Long links wrap badly.
03

Vitals

Answers "what is wrong with this account" first

Artifacts are not the first question. When you open an account it is usually because someone says it does not work, so this opens on a diagnosis: runway and quota as rings, then one line per membership with a status light and a verdict in words.

The fixture proves the point. IKEv2 has moved 0 B since it was created, which almost always means the client never completed a handshake, and GRE is switched off on that inbound while the account itself is enabled. Both are invisible in every other design here; this one says them out loud before you have clicked anything.

More Information is the row opening in place. Click a membership and it expands under its own line with the technical readout and its artifact, without covering the diagnosis that made you click it.
Try: the two rows with warnings then any healthy one
CostsBreaks
Front-end, plus the verdict rules: never-connected, disabled-here, depleted, expiring, no-artifact. Every input already exists on the row. A wrong verdict is worse than none, and "never connected" has innocent explanations. Handing links to a customer becomes the second job, behind a tab.
04

Composer

You opened this to give somebody something

Most of the time the reason to open an account is to send the customer their access. So this is not a viewer, it is a builder: tick which memberships to include, choose who it is for, and watch the message assemble itself on the right. Then copy it, download it, or switch to a QR sheet.

It makes explicit what Export TXT does blindly today. Customer strips quota, expiry and anything internal and writes install-ready instructions; Operator keeps everything. GRE is offered but cannot be ticked, because there is nothing to put in a message, and the view says so rather than silently dropping it.

More Information is the inspector. A row's magnifier opens the technical readout beside the picker. It is deliberately not part of the composed output, which is the whole point of separating them.
Try: untick memberships switch Customer / Operator then QR sheet
CostsBreaks
Front-end, plus a second renderer for the customer voice and its wording in 13 locales. The most opinionated design here: it assumes the errand. Someone who just wanted to look at an account has to read a form first. Two output voices is two things to keep true.
05

History

Support questions are almost always about time

"It stopped working on Tuesday." No other design here can answer that. This one is a time axis: created, first connection, traffic as it accrued, last seen, and the expiry it is running at. Under it, one lane per membership showing when each was added and when it was last used.

Read against the fixture it tells a story the others cannot. IKEv2 was added three weeks ago and its lane is empty for its whole length. GRE stops dead where it was switched off. The VLESS lane runs to today. That is a diagnosis you get by looking, not by reasoning.

More Information is a lane, opened. Click any lane and its detail appears below the axis: the technical readout, its artifact, and its own first-and-last-seen, without leaving the timeline that gave it context.
Try: click a lane compare the IKEv2 and GRE lanes with the VLESS one
CostsBreaks
The only one needing backend work. Per-membership first-seen and a traffic series do not exist; the panel stores counters and a last-online, not history. Without that history the axis is three points and a guess. Handing over links is an afterthought here, and a customer never needs this view at all.
06

Choosing

They answer different questions, so pick by question
DesignAnswersMore Information becomesNeeds backendGood for
01 Wallet"What does this customer have?"The back of a cardNoHanding things over, visually
02 Manifest"Show me everything, now"A verb that filtersNoOperators, tickets, speed
03 Vitals"Why is it not working?"A row opening in placeNoSupport, triage
04 Composer"Send them their access"An inspector beside the formNoOnboarding, resellers
05 History"When did it break?"A lane, openedYesHard support cases

If one has to ship: 03 Vitals, with 02's readout underneath it. Vitals is the only one that opens by answering the question that actually makes someone open an account, and it earns its keep on the fixture by catching two real faults that every other design renders invisibly. Manifest is the cheapest thing in this document and loses nothing by being the body of the Vitals detail, so the pair costs barely more than either alone.

If two can ship: add 04 Composer as the export path. It replaces Export TXT and Export PDF rather than sitting beside them, so it removes a blind action instead of adding a view. Wallet and History are the two I would not build first: Wallet is the most attractive and the least useful, and History is the only one that cannot be built at all without storing per-membership history the panel does not currently keep.

Still undecided, and it is a real decision. Whether Account Info may show quota, expiry and traffic when it is being used to hand something to a customer. 04 makes it a toggle. 01 and 05 would leak it. 03 is built entirely out of it. Whatever ships needs an answer.
